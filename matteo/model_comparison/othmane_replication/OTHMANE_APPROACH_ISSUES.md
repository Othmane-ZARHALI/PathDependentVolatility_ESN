# Issues and limitations in Othmane's nine-asset joint calibration

Audit date: 2026-09-30. Scope: the historical nine-asset study in
`SimultanuousAllFeatures/`, especially `joint_calibration.py`, its saved fit,
and `joint_calibration_protocol.tex`. This note distinguishes defects in the
checked-in entry point from limitations of the statistical comparison. It does
not establish that every defect affected the archived parameter file.

## Confirmed issues in the checked-in calibration code

1. **The entry point cannot start as checked in.** `starting_point()` returns
   five coordinates, while `calibrate_joint()` unpacks six and supplies six
   lower and upper bounds. Calling it with that starting point raises a bounds
   shape error. See `joint_calibration.py:318-321,350-351,388,452-470` and
   [the source audit](AUDIT.md).

2. **The invalid-region residual has the wrong length.** The ordinary objective
   contains 59 entries: 1 roughness, 40 leverage, 6 absolute-return ACF,
   6 squared-return ACF, 5 Zumbach, and 1 kurtosis. The `kappa0 >= 0` branch
   returns 53 entries, so reaching it would give the optimizer an inconsistent
   residual shape. Under the current orientation and positive rough scale, the
   audit found that this branch appears unreachable; it is still an incorrect
   fallback. See `joint_calibration.py:234-273,363-368` and [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md).

3. **Stage 2 uses the wrong `alpha` unless it is passed explicitly.** After
   fitting `alpha` in Stage 1, the checked-in call to
   `bisect_m1_for_kurtosis()` omits it. The function then uses its default
   `alpha=1`, although the saved fits have smaller values. A controlled SP500
   rerun at fixed archived H, rough scale, and decay rates gave `m1=0.196`
   with fitted `alpha=0.2204`, versus `m1=0.059` with the default. Evaluating
   the latter at fitted alpha gave excess kurtosis 5.13 versus the historical
   target 10.31. This demonstrates a material sensitivity of the *checked-in
   call*; it does not show how the archived `m1` was actually obtained. See
   `joint_calibration.py:276,478-488`, [the controlled audit](stage2_alpha_sp500_001.json),
   and [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).

4. **The checked-in serializer cannot produce the archived final file.** It
   omits `alpha_fit`, which is present in `joint_calibration_final.json`. Along
   with the five-versus-six-coordinate error, this means the saved final
   parameters cannot be regenerated by running the checked-in entry point
   unchanged. Their exact code provenance remains uncertain. See
   `joint_calibration.py:486-491` and [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md). A corrected copy is
   [here](joint_calibration_reviewed.py), but a full nine-asset refit has not
   yet been completed.

## Measurement and inference limitations

5. **Historical and simulated variance are observed differently.** The real
   roughness, leverage, and Zumbach targets use a same-day Garman–Klass OHLC
   variance proxy. The ESN objective applies these statistics to its raw
   latent variance; its returns are driven by a smoothed EWMA variance. Thus
   even a small residual can partly reflect matching different measurement
   objects. A matched test would generate simulated OHLC and apply the same
   variance proxy and alignment to data and simulation. See
   `matteo/preprocessing/market_data.py:104-126` (relative to repository root),
   `joint_calibration.py:176-195`, and [PLAN.md](PLAN.md).

6. **The roughness estimators use different lag bands.** The historical target
   uses lags 2–40 to avoid the noisy one-day OHLC proxy; the model objective
   uses 1–40. This may be defensible, but the two reported H values are not
   computed by identical finite-sample rules. The [lag sensitivity audit](roughness_lag_sensitivity_001.json)
   recomputes both sides on common bands. See
   `matteo/preprocessing/hurst.py:11` (relative to repository root) and
   `joint_calibration.py:85,190`.

7. **The objective weights determine much of the ranking.** H has a factor
   of 3 in the residual, hence a factor of 9 in its squared-error contribution.
   Leverage lag L is multiplied by `(L+1)^-2` before squaring, so later lags
   have little numerical influence. All 40 leverage lags are treated as
   separate normalized residuals even though neighboring estimates are
   correlated. These are specified design choices, not implementation bugs;
   conclusions about “overall fit” should show the block contributions and
   sensitivity to weighting. See `joint_calibration.py:225-273` and
   [the score breakdown](plots/nine_asset_scores.csv).

8. **The reported historical fit is in sample.** The saved per-asset
   parameters were calibrated on the full historical targets. Performance on
   those same targets is a fit diagnostic, not evidence of performance on
   later, unseen market data. The pre/post-2015 [readout screen](RESULTS.md)
   cannot turn the archived ESN into a held-out comparator, because its
   parameters already used the later years. A direct predictive comparison
   requires the same training cutoff and later evaluation for both models.

9. **Simulation bands do not quantify uncertainty in the market targets.**
   `build_confidence_band()` takes 5th and 95th percentiles across simulated
   paths at fixed parameters. This describes simulation variation. It does
   not account for finite historical samples, correlated lag statistics,
   parameter uncertainty, or calibration across alternative starts. A data
   block bootstrap and repeated fits would address different questions. See
   `joint_calibration.py:411-428` and [PLAN.md](PLAN.md).

10. **The search is narrow and tied to one simulation realization per run.**
    The checked-in entry point uses one starting point per asset and fixed
    simulation seeds within each optimizer run. Common random numbers reduce
    gradient noise, but the resulting optimum can depend on that realization.
    Several architecture and readout choices remain fixed rather than being
    calibrated. Othmane's own report identifies the seed dependence and fixed
    choices as limitations. See `joint_calibration.py:63-93,331-346,452-470`
    and `joint_calibration_protocol.tex:515-540`.

## Reported fit gaps, not code defects

The original report itself identifies RUSSELL2000 as a poor fit on both
return-persistence curves and Zumbach, and notes that Zumbach magnitudes
often undershoot even when their shapes look right. These are empirical gaps
of the saved configuration, not proof that the ESN family cannot fit them.
See `joint_calibration_protocol.tex:510-528`.

## What the audit did and did not establish

The six saved historical target JSON files exactly matched an in-memory
rebuild from the local raw OHLC inputs; there is no identified target-file
discrepancy ([target audit](target_audit_001.json)). The archived parameters
can be simulated and scored on fresh seeds. The checked-in original entry
point cannot regenerate them unchanged, and a complete multi-start,
matched-observation, held-out historical recalibration has not been run.

The “M2 synthetic anchor” in [our comparison](RESULTS.md) is a separate
limitation of **our comparison**: it was selected in synthetic experiments,
then scored against Othmane's historical targets without a comparable
per-asset historical fit. It should not be attributed to Othmane's method.
