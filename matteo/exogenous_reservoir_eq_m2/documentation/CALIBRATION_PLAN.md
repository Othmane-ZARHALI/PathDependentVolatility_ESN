# One-date P/Q calibration plan for the exogenous-reservoir model

Status: implementation plan, revised 30 September 2026 after consistency review and the SPX stability study. Follow [the option preprocessing plan](OPTION_PREPROCESSING_PLAN.md) first. This is a handoff specification, not a claim of an achieved fit. The primary pilot is tempered Gaussian-reservoir M2, with no echo or orthogonal loading. The model PDFs in this directory define the equations; their suggested calibration sequence is treated as research guidance rather than an instruction that overrides the observed data.

## Objective, information boundary, and claims

Find model configurations that fit the 2026-02-19 SPX/VIX option snapshot and its four VIX forwards while remaining compatible with SPX physical-measure statistics. Compare fitted prices with actual bid/ask intervals and show the physical-fit trade-off. This plan focuses on **one-date feasibility**. A single date cannot establish across-date parameter stability, forecast quality, or a uniquely estimated variance-risk premium. Additional-date data acquisition is a later research stage, outside this implementation plan.

The available `data/processed/bench9_real_*.json` files hold historical point targets, including SPX Hurst, leverage, two Taylor curves, Zumbach and excess kurtosis. The underlying SPX OHLC workbook ends 2023-11-14, more than two years before the option date. Do not treat its final observed state as the February 2026 state or label a state inferred only from one option cross-section as filtered from history. The other eight assets are architecture robustness examples, not substitutes for SPX uncertainty bands.

The [SPX physical-stability study](../experiments/physical_stability_001/RESULTS.md) finds negative leverage and positive volatility clustering in every studied five- and ten-year window, but their magnitudes vary. Excess kurtosis differs substantially across disjoint periods and rises sharply in windows containing extreme episodes. Roughness and the daily-OHLC Zumbach proxy are sensitive to bootstrap block length; the recorded-open convention also appears to change over the sample. The annually stepped rolling windows are dependent descriptions of *when* estimates move, not additional independent moment observations. These findings rule out using the nine historical point values as one precise, constant P target vector. They do not by themselves prove that the model's structural parameters vary: a fixed-parameter stochastic model can produce variable finite-window estimates. The distinction requires a matched simulation check below.

4. Each run writes a configuration and manifest containing source hashes, model/architecture version, train/validation split, free/fixed parameters, bounds, all numerical budgets, seeds, loss scales and objective weights. No result should depend on an unrecorded constant in a script.

**Time convention.** The pilot Q engine uses ACT/365 elapsed years for diffusion time, inverse-year OU rates and $\Delta=30/365$. A budget of (n) steps per trading day denotes a maximum step of $1/(252n)$ years, not an instruction to round quoted maturities to trading-day counts. Insert each exact quoted expiry, every VIX-window endpoint and curve knot into the integration grid; recompute exact OU transition covariance for fractional steps. The P measurement grid retains 252 daily observations per model year. This is an explicit homogeneous-clock approximation; calendar-aware overnight/weekend seasonality is a separate sensitivity and must not silently rescale rates or variance. Record both grids and verify that their step lengths sum to the requested horizons. Missing intraday timestamps remain a settlement limitation.

For the first one-date run, fit the 29-, 57-, 211- and 302-day SPX expiries and all four VIX expiries. Reserve the entire 119-day SPX expiry for final maturity testing, excluding it from primary curve construction and model selection. Exclude the 1-day SPX expiry because minute-level timestamp/settlement metadata are absent. Treat the 666-day SPX expiry as a maturity diagnostic and expiries beyond two years as extrapolation diagnostics. Within each fitted expiry, assign zero-based sorted eligible OTM strike ranks congruent to 4 modulo 10 to model-selection validation and ranks congruent to 9 modulo 10 to final testing; fit all other valid two-sided OTM quotes without an extra moneyness cutoff. Keep rejected and held-out quotes in reporting. If the training-only curve cannot be justified, use the declared nuisance family or report the limitation; do not silently train on the 119-day expiry or held-out strikes. These choices are **pilot defaults**, to freeze in the run configuration before optimisation, not conclusions about which maturities are economically important.

Illustrative *search bounds*, not empirical priors: $c_C\in[0.05,0.80]$, $q_Z\in[0.02,0.25]$, $\beta\in[0.05,3.5]$, $q_J\in[0.002,0.30]$, $\beta_J\in[0,0.5]$, $\rho_F\in[0.50,0.99]$, and $\rho_J\in[0.10,0.90]$. Use log coordinates for positive curvatures. Fix $\underline v=10^{-4}$ annualised variance, $\theta=0.35$, $\kappa=6$, and start with (L=5.5); profile $L\in\{3.5,5.5,6.5\}$. The floor and equity premium are placeholders inherited from the research configuration; recheck their effect in sensitivity runs. Use unspanned-price scenarios $(\gamma_{B_F},\gamma_{B_J})\in\{(0,0),(0.25,0),(-0.25,0),(0,0.25),(0,-0.25)\}$; the other unspanned prices remain zero. If a result sits on a bound, expand that bound once and report the profile before calling it identified. The Stage-3 local box is an additional warm-start region, not the only admissible domain.

## Parameter hierarchy for this pilot

| Class | Primary treatment | Sensitivity / later panel study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model cell | M2: `echo_loading=orthogonal_curvature=0` | M2+O, M3 and M4 only after a material model-selection validation residual and paired ablation. |
| OU counts, grid end points, powers, fixed echo seed | Frozen architecture for each run | Repeat a few predeclared architectures; do not optimise echo seeds on the snapshot. |
| $c_C,q_Z,\beta,q_J,\beta_J$ | Shared P/Q readout parameters, fitted within physical constraints | Report profiles and correlations; preserve $q_Z,q_J\ge0$. |
| $\rho_F,\rho_J$ | Small outer grid or broad regularised structural fit | Both affect P facts and Q options; rebuilding correlated states is permitted from the same primitive randomness. |
| Cap $L,\kappa$, floor $\underline v$ | Fix one documented admissible baseline, profile (L) over a few values | Report P/Q cap fractions and upper-tail sensitivity. Do not use (L) solely as a numerical repair. |
| Equity premium $\theta$ and unspanned $\gamma$ | Fix/strongly restrict $\theta$; start with zero unspanned prices, then a small declared scenario grid | One option date cannot support one risk price per OU coordinate. Never set the effective feedback/spike Q shift to zero merely because unspanned premia are zero: it still contains $\rho_b\theta$. |
| $\xi_0(t)$, forwards, rates, dividends | Date-specific inputs or a separately profiled curve with uncertainty | The same SPX quotes used to construct $\xi_0$ are not independent residual observations. |
| Current reservoir state (X_0) | Latent date-specific state with a prior or a low-dimensional restriction | Filter from current history if acquired; then revisit risk-premium inference. |
| Bootstrap settings, split rule, regularisation, objective weights | Estimation hyperparameters fixed before final validation | Select through training-only sensitivity; one-date holdout is not time validation. |
| Step size, path counts, quadrature order | Numerical controls selected by convergence | Never use discretisation error to improve fit. |

The default code parameterisation may be retained for the first implementation. A better-conditioned optional experiment uses $\ell_F=-2q_Z\beta$, $\ell_J=-2q_J\beta_J$, with centred features $q_Z(Y^2-1)+\ell_FY+q_J(F^2-1)+\ell_JF$. This **replaces** the vertex coordinates. To preserve exactly the original normalised tempered model, transform the cap from (L) to (L-d), where $d=q_Z(1+\beta^2)+q_J(1+\beta_J^2)$, because $h_L(\widetilde\eta+d)=d+h_{L-d}(\widetilde\eta)$. Respect the original sign/zero-curvature domain; allowing $\ell\ne0$ at (q=0) extends the model and is a separate challenger. Add a unit test for pointwise equality of the normalised variance before adopting this parameterisation.

## Work package A: reconstruct physical targets with uncertainty

Produce SPX-only target vectors with metadata for at least: finite-band log-volatility structure functions and fitted roughness; volatility clustering; lagged return-to-variance correlation; Pearson and rank Zumbach at explicitly matched horizons; absolute-return and squared-return ACFs; tail quantiles/kurtosis at declared aggregation horizons; and near-zero return ACF. The provisional empirical hierarchy differs from the model's mechanism priorities: use negative leverage and positive clustering, including their lag profiles, as the strongest candidate P constraints once measurement is matched. Evaluate tails across declared calm and stressed windows and aggregation horizons, not through one full-sample kurtosis. Treat return ACF mainly as a near-zero diagnostic, Taylor as secondary, and OHLC-based roughness and Zumbach as exploratory until intraday realised variance and source conventions are checked. Retain the raw curves rather than reducing every fact to one scalar. Freeze the actual fitted subset only after proxy and uncertainty checks, before viewing option fit quality.

Use a time-block or stationary bootstrap on the **SPX time series**, including the same volatility estimator in every bootstrap replication. Choose and report block-length sensitivity around the longest material dependence lag. Estimate a covariance for the compact target vector, regularise small eigenvalues and retain individual percentile intervals. Do not treat 40 adjacent lag points or overlapping Zumbach windows as independent data. If a covariance estimate is unstable, reduce the number of curve knots or fit regularised functional components rather than silently using a diagonal matrix.

Resample aligned daily tuples of close-to-close return and within-day log ratios $\log(H/O),\log(L/O),\log(C/O)$ together, preserving their joint dependence within blocks. Recompute Garman–Klass from those ratios in every replication. If the shared measurement interface requires OHLC levels, reconstruct internally consistent synthetic bars: advance the close using the sampled return, infer the open from the sampled close/open ratio, and recover high/low from their open ratios. Never concatenate original price-level blocks and recompute returns across their joins: that introduces artificial jumps. Exclude lagged pairs and Zumbach windows crossing artificial block joins, as in the stability study; record this convention and test that joins create no returns other than the sampled ones.

The exploratory study used 1,000 replications per disjoint five-year period with 126-day primary blocks and 63-/252-day sensitivities; its rolling windows used 500 replications and 126-day blocks. For the final compact target vector, use at least 1,000 replications, predeclare a primary block length, and repeat 63-, 126- and 252-day cases where the window supports enough blocks. Longer blocks can be examined on longer samples, but a three-year window gives very few independent long blocks. Freeze the chosen statistic vector and primary block length before viewing option fit quality. If block choices yield incompatible physical confidence regions, report the sensitivity and avoid a single hard P threshold. Construct a compact set of predeclared lags (for example 2, 5, 10, 20, 40 trading days) for multivariate fitting; preserve the full original curves for independent reporting.

The historical inputs combine close-to-close returns and same-day Garman–Klass OHLC variance. Implement a comparable observation layer for simulated P paths: simulated closes, opens, highs and lows from a sufficiently fine grid, followed by the **same** return and Garman–Klass functions. Check time-step convergence of the OHLC proxy. Until that layer is validated, mark variance-proxy-based Hurst, leverage, clustering and Zumbach constraints as exploratory; returns-only facts may still be used. The index's recorded open equals the previous close on about 96% of days in 1999-2003 and about 0.1% in 2019-2023. Audit this source change, compare the Garman–Klass result with high/low-only and overnight-gap sensitivities, and do not interpret an open-dependent time trend as economic without a comparable source. Separate overnight and regular-session behaviour if the source OHLC convention allows it. The documented target should state whether an intraday realised-variance estimate later replaces this proxy.

Because the historical window spans decades, do not drive every P simulation with the single 2026 forward-variance curve. Use declared historical regime windows and observed variance-level anchors or level nuisance parameters for those windows. Compare scale-free statistics where justified, and compare tail/level statistics only under an explicit volatility-level convention. Keep the 2026 $\xi_0$ input for 2026 Q pricing. Record the dependence of simulated P variance on Q normalisation and risk-price scenarios:

\[
E^P[V_t]=\underline v+(\xi_0(t)-\underline v)
\frac{E^P[G(\eta_t)]}{E^Q[G(\eta_t)]}.
\]

Compare observed rolling estimates with repeated *full-history-length* P simulations from each candidate, processed through the same sampling dates or declared regime durations, OHLC observation layer, estimators and rolling windows. Compare the distribution of rolling ranges, episode jumps, lag curves and calm/stress contrasts, with simulation uncertainty. The additional 28 three-year windows resolve episode transitions more sharply but have less precise tail and asymmetry estimates. Do not fit the 26 five-year, 21 ten-year or 28 three-year windows as independent residuals or infer nonstationary parameters from visual changes alone. If the observed variation lies outside the model's simulation envelope, investigate source changes and variance-level conventions first, then test whether a missing state or regime mechanism is needed. A time-varying structural parameter is a later hypothesis to validate, not a default repair.

**Acceptance:** target estimates reproduce the existing point targets when conventions match; bootstrap intervals and covariance are finite; disjoint-window heterogeneity and rolling dependence are reported; simulated and observed statistics pass identical measurement code; the matched rolling simulation check is recorded; and all lag/frequency/sample definitions are in a manifest.

## Work package B: complete and validate the M2 pricing engine

Implement pricing in new `esn_eq/pricing.py` and `esn_eq/vix_pricing.py` modules, with a small independent reference pricer for tests. Keep state generation, variance readout, and payoff evaluation separate so cached primitive normals and OU paths can be reused. Price all strikes in one terminal-path ensemble per maturity; do not resimulate for each strike or reuse final stock paths after changing readout parameters.

**Initial state and normalisation.** For a *specified* current state (X_0=x_0), define the dated normaliser $M_Q^0(u)=E_0^Q[G(\eta_u)\mid X_0=x_0]$ and $V_u=\underline v+[\xi_0(u)-\underline v]G(\eta_u)/M_Q^0(u)$. Re-estimate (M_Q^0) for every candidate structural/risk-premium/readout vector. The current `readout.normalizer()` averages the same Q ensemble it later verifies; that equality is an in-sample construction, not an independent integration-error test. Do not average stationary initial states when claiming to price conditional on the date's state. If the state is represented by a posterior distribution, state clearly whether prices and the normaliser condition on a point estimate or average over the posterior, and use the same convention throughout.

**SPX.** Simulate exact correlated OU/equity increments from the dated state under Q, with adapted start-of-step variance. In forward units compute $Y_T=S_T/F^{SPX}_0(T)$, with $E_0^Q[Y_T]=1$, and $C^U=F^{SPX}_0(T)E[(Y_T-K/F^{SPX}_0(T))^+]$; use the analogous put payoff. Include deterministic carry to reconcile spot and each market SPX forward. Use common random numbers across candidate readouts, antithetics/conditional payoff smoothing if helpful, and fresh seeds for confirmation. Report price standard errors by strike with shared-path covariance where needed.

**VIX definition, futures and options.** For $\Delta=30/365$, use the documented continuous-window approximation

\[
\mathrm{VIX}_T^2=\frac{100^2}{\Delta}
\int_T^{T+\Delta} E_T^Q[V_u]\,du,
\quad F^{VIX}_{model}(T)=E_0^Q[\mathrm{VIX}_T],
\quad C^{U,VIX}(T,K)=E_0^Q[(\mathrm{VIX}_T-K)^+].
\]

The futures price is the mean of **VIX**, not the square root of mean VIX squared. Model VIX option prices use absolute quoted strikes and the *model* VIX distribution; put prices use $E_0^Q[(K-\mathrm{VIX}_T)^+]$. The market VIX forward is a separate calibration residual, not an imposed replacement for the model futures price. The approximation omits exact settlement and Cboe strike-level conventions; record this model-basis difference and test its effect when more data are available. In particular, actual VIX derivatives settle to a Special Opening Quotation based on constituent SPX opening prices; see the [Cboe settlement explanation](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/vix/faqs). More numerical samples cannot supply missing settlement metadata or an auction model.

**Two supported numerical engines.** Implement nested Monte Carlo first as an independent reference and a valid selectable pricing engine for both futures and options. Add Gaussian quadrature as an alternative M2 engine; select the production engine by measured cost at matched accuracy, not by a claim that either removes the continuous-window approximation. Record `vix_engine = nested_mc | gaussian_quadrature` and all associated budgets.

For nested Monte Carlo, draw (N) outer Q states $X_T^{(n)}$ from the dated initial-state convention. For each state draw (M) conditionally independent OU continuations over $[T,T+\Delta]$ and compute

\[
\widehat A_T^{(n)}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M\int_T^{T+\Delta}
\left[\underline v+(\xi_0(u)-\underline v)
\frac{G(\eta_u^{(n,m)})}{M_Q^0(u)}\right]du,
\qquad \widehat X_T^{(n)}=100\sqrt{\widehat A_T^{(n)}/\Delta}.
\]

Average $\widehat X_T^{(n)}$, $(\widehat X_T^{(n)}-K)^+$ and $(K-\widehat X_T^{(n)})^+$ over outer samples to estimate the future, call and put prices. Reuse each outer VIX estimate for all strikes at that expiry. Keep the **date-zero** normaliser $M_Q^0(u)$ fixed across outer and inner draws for a candidate; never renormalise separately at time (T). An independently seeded normaliser estimate or validated quadrature is required, with its numerical error assessed separately. Stock paths need not be simulated on the inner branch for this variance-functional definition.

Average integrated variance across inner draws **before** taking its square root. A single realised future-variance path is not the conditional expectation defining VIX. Finite inner sampling generally biases the nonlinear outer prices: with an unbiased positive inner estimate and otherwise exact inputs, concavity gives downward bias for the futures estimator; there is no general common bias sign for the option payoffs. Increasing outer paths alone does not remove inner bias. Refine inner sample count, outer sample count and time integration separately, use independent inner streams across outer states for the standard outer standard-error estimator, and report nested bias separately from sampling standard errors. Shared normaliser uncertainty requires independent normaliser/run replicates. These requirements follow the general pitfalls of [nested Monte Carlo estimation](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/rainforth18a.html).

For Gaussian quadrature in M2, conditional on full (X_T), only the projections (U_u=(C_u,Y_u,F_u)) enter $G(\eta_u)$; they have a joint three-dimensional Gaussian law. Evaluate $E_T^Q[G(\eta_u)]$ by adaptive or converged three-dimensional Gaussian quadrature, then integrate over the 30-day window. Use the full OU state to compute the conditional projection mean; the three projections alone need not be Markov. Calculate (M_Q^0(u)) from the same exact Gaussian projection law conditional on (X_0), with independent quadrature or a separately seeded reference. Handle the zero-covariance endpoint $u=T$ directly. If quadrature is inaccurate in cap/tail regimes, use nested Monte Carlo, controlled sparse grids or a validated surrogate; do not silently clamp an invalid result. Optional echo/orthogonal extensions require their own valid conditional-state treatment; the three-dimensional M2 reduction does not automatically apply.

For either engine, require the preprocessing second-moment gate and verify
$E_0^Q[\mathrm{VIX}_T^2]=10^4[W(T+\Delta)-W(T)]/\Delta$ and
$(F^{VIX}_{model}(T))^2\le E_0^Q[\mathrm{VIX}_T^2]$ within separately reported numerical error. Recheck input compatibility whenever a nuisance curve changes; this is a necessary restriction on attainable VIX prices, independent of the numerical engine.

**Required numerical tests:** exact OU means/covariances and deterministic P-to-Q shift, including fractional steps; equality of reused primitive draws under readout changes; $E^Q[Y_T]=1$ within independent simulation error; $E_0^Q[V_u]=\xi_0(u)$ on an *independent* integration sample; finite positive (M_Q); quadrature order and maturity-grid convergence; nested-MC inner/outer/time-grid refinement and agreement between engines on fixed synthetic parameter/state points within sampling error plus assessed bias; call monotonicity/convexity and put-call parity of model prices; VIX second-moment/tower and Jensen checks; and consistency of (F^{VIX}_{model}) with the simulated VIX distribution. Include a constant-variance fixture with deterministic VIX, an exact conditional endpoint fixture, and several tail states. Passing the second-moment check alone does not certify nested futures or option prices because inner sampling can leave that moment correct while biasing nonlinear payoffs.

## Work package C: dated state and measure-change scenarios

Primary data limitation: there are no observed 2024–2026 SPX returns in this repository, so (X_0) cannot be filtered to the option date. Treat the state as a **penalised latent variable**, not as known or as an independent free value for every OU coordinate. A first restriction is to fit (u_0=(Y_0,F_0,C_0)), map it into the full OU state by the minimum-Mahalanobis-norm conditional Gaussian mean, and penalise (u_0) under the stationary P projection covariance. If H is the 3-by-N projection matrix and Σ is the stationary P covariance, use `x0 = Σ H^T (H Σ H^T)^(-1) u0` and the prior penalty `u0^T (H Σ H^T)^(-1) u0`. Check HΣH^T conditioning. Then test price sensitivity to residual full-state directions or integrate a declared conditional-state distribution with covariance `Σ - Σ H^T (H Σ H^T)^(-1) H Σ`. If results change materially, the restricted-state pilot is inconclusive. Once recent returns/RV are obtained, replace this proxy with a chronological filter and compare results.

Use a small risk-premium scenario set. Scenario 0 has unspanned Brownian prices equal to zero while keeping the equity price equal to the declared $\theta$; the four other scenarios vary the sign of one feedback or one spike unspanned price within documented research bounds. Apply `states.primitive_risk_prices()` and `q_state_paths()` consistently, including $\gamma_F=\rho_F\theta+\sqrt{1-\rho_F^2}\gamma_{B_F}$ and its spike analogue. Do not identify an independent premium for every slow coordinate from one snapshot. For every risk-price scenario, recompute Q means, normalisation, all option prices and the P loss. Report profile curves rather than selecting a point solely by the smallest option loss.

Use the state regulariser $R_{state}=\lambda_{state}u_0^\top(H\Sigma H^\top)^{-1}u_0/3$ in both constrained and unconstrained fits. Pilot default: $\lambda_{state}=0.1$, with predeclared 0.01 and 1.0 sensitivities. This is an optimisation regularisation choice on the family-averaged loss scale, not a calibrated posterior probability. Record the coefficient and separate state penalty from market residuals. Any nuisance-curve regulariser must likewise have an explicit frozen coefficient and be reported separately.

## Work package D: objectives and fit sequence

Use the quote **interval** as the primary observation. For an undiscounted model price (p_i) and market interval ([b_i,a_i]), define the signed distance (d_i=p_i-b_i) if (p_i<b_i), zero if $b_i\le p_i\le a_i$, and (p_i-a_i) if (p_i>a_i). Divide by a predeclared fixed market scale; assess numerical error separately. Use equal total weight per instrument/maturity block, then report errors separately for SPX puts, SPX calls, VIX puts, VIX calls and each maturity. A *small, labelled* mid-price tie-breaker may distinguish many interval-feasible candidates; report results without it too. Invalid/nonfinite prices are evaluation failures with explicit penalties/status, never zero residuals.

Pilot loss defaults, all configurable and recorded: `s_i = max((a_i-b_i)/2, 1e-5*F_i)`, where `F_i` is the relevant SPX or VIX **market** forward. Freeze these scales before search; candidate-specific Monte Carlo standard errors never enter the denominator. Within each `(instrument, expiry)`, average `(d_i/s_i)^2` over fitted quotes; then average expiry means within each instrument. Set the interval-loss weight to one for SPX and one for VIX. Use no midpoint tie-breaker in the primary fit; rerun with a small predeclared coefficient such as 0.05 on the analogous scaled mid residual as an identification sensitivity. Do not let the quote count or very small IV spreads set family weights implicitly. Report fraction inside intervals alongside the loss, the preprocessing arbitrage-free minimum-violation benchmark, and every flagged infeasible expiry. If the model VIX mean differs from the market forward used in that benchmark, recompute the benchmark with that mean before comparing losses; parity must use the same mean. Keep the original market-forward audit too.

The four VIX forwards receive four level residuals, not one residual per VIX option row. The user believes these are traded futures quotes; verify that provenance before calling them independent futures observations. Their bid/ask uncertainty is absent from the CSV. For a pilot, average squared forward errors in VIX points using a declared 0.5-point scale, and repeat with 0.25 and 1.0 points. Give this family the same initial total weight as either option family, then also fit with its weight zero. The option IVs were quoted using these forwards, which may correlate option-price and forward errors; do not interpret the scales as measured bid/ask uncertainty. Report model-implied forward errors and VIX convexity. Likewise, do not score a derived SPX variance curve and its source SPX option prices as independent information. Use the curve as a constraint/input with propagated uncertainty or profile it as a nuisance fit.

Construct the primary P residual only from predeclared, source-comparable summaries whose interpretation survives the proxy and block-length checks. Use uncertainty-aware soft constraints on leverage and clustering profiles; do not require every historical window to hit one exact magnitude. Compare episode-sensitive tails and the full rolling-variation diagnostics separately with matched P simulations. A sign constraint or explicit practical tolerance may be more defensible than a narrow confidence ellipsoid for an unstable level. Treat exploratory Hurst, Zumbach and Taylor measurements as reported validation diagnostics unless later data support promotion. Do not count correlated lag points, overlapping windows or the nine statistics as independent observations.

For the selected compact vector, use $r_P=m_P^{model}-m_P^{data}$ and a regularised covariance $\Sigma_P=\Sigma_{data}+\Sigma_{MC}$, estimated with appropriate time blocks and independent simulation seeds. Here $\Sigma_{data}$ is sampling uncertainty for the declared comparable span, not a license to pool empirically different periods. If a common population target is still proposed across disjoint periods, estimate and report between-period dispersion separately after accounting for within-period uncertainty, state the random-effects assumption and profile the result by period. Treat volatility-proxy differences as specification sensitivities, not automatically as independent Gaussian noise added to $\Sigma_P$. Start with $L_P=r_P^\top\Sigma_P^{-1}r_P$, but calibrate its threshold from **joint data and model-estimation errors** for the same chosen vector and span. Generate independent centred historical-bootstrap errors $e_{data}^{(b)}$ and centred model-simulation errors $e_{MC}^{(b)}$ at the actual P sample length and path budget. Use $z_b=e_{MC}^{(b)}-e_{data}^{(b)}$ and take the 95th percentile of $z_b^\top\Sigma_P^{-1}z_b$, with the covariance and regularisation frozen while forming that reference distribution. Report 90th- and 99th-percentile sensitivities. Estimate model-error replicates independently of the draws producing the candidate residual; document centring and any finite-sample estimator bias. This threshold describes sampling and simulation error conditional on the chosen target definition; it is not a test that all historical regimes share one value.

If model-error covariance materially changes across candidates or budgets, recompute both covariance and the joint reference distribution consistently; never add $\Sigma_{MC}$ to the loss while calibrating its threshold from data-only deviations. A data-only bootstrap is allowed only after a declared negligible-MC gate, for example $\lambda_{max}(\Sigma_{data,reg}^{-1/2}\Sigma_{MC}\Sigma_{data,reg}^{-1/2})\le0.01$; then omit $\Sigma_{MC}$ from both the loss covariance and threshold calibration. Refine simulation rather than certify P feasibility through large numerical uncertainty; final classifications must be stable under larger independent P budgets. Also report individual empirical intervals; one severe miss must remain visible even when a total loss is small.

Recommended sequence:

1. Freeze preprocessing outputs, architecture candidates, exact parameter bounds, seeds, fixed loss scales, regularisation coefficients and train/validation/final-test roles. Require the variance-floor and cross-market compatibility gates, or explicitly label unresolved input incompatibility. Get a numerical reference fit of the pricing engine on synthetic surfaces before touching the real snapshot.
2. Space-fill the M2 P parameter box with Sobol or Latin hypercube draws and several $\rho_F,\rho_J$ outer scenarios. Retain all candidates and P-feasible/near-feasible regions; use the Stage-3 local box only as one starting region. For representative candidates, compare observed rolling variability with the model's full-length simulation envelope before interpreting the historical drift as structural. If no candidate is feasible under the predeclared, source-comparable primary P vector, inspect measurement conventions, cap, rate-grid bounds and target compatibility before fitting Q. An exploratory proxy failure alone must not reject a candidate.
3. For each retained structural/risk-price scenario, optimise shared readout parameters plus restricted current state against SPX prices, VIX forwards and VIX-option prices, **subject to** the chosen P-feasibility rule. The primary objective is $L_{SPX}+L_{VIX}+L_{Futures}+R_{state}+R_{curve}$, with $R_{curve}=0$ for a fixed input curve and an explicitly configured penalty for a nuisance curve. Recompute P diagnostics after any shared parameter or risk-price change. Use multiple initial points, deterministic quadrature where helpful, fixed common random numbers for noisy prices, and the finite squared interval-distance objective. Keep a coarse-to-fine numerical budget and rank candidates only after reevaluation satisfying the same numerical accuracy tolerances; equal path counts alone do not establish equal accuracy.
4. Generate a P/Q Pareto table by repeating the fit across predeclared P tolerance levels and instrument-family weights. A no-feasible-fit outcome is a result. Do not free optional components or overwrite the P readout merely to erase one smile residual.
5. Re-evaluate finalists with fresh random seeds, independent normaliser calculations, finer time steps and refined quadrature or nested-MC budgets. Use model-selection validation strikes for selection, then freeze all choices before evaluating final-test strikes and the 119-day SPX maturity. Retain the full ledger, not only the winner.

Pilot search budget: begin with 256 scrambled Sobol structural/readout candidates per architecture and five declared unspanned-price scenarios. Retain the best P-feasible and near-feasible regions, then use at least eight diverse starting points for the Q optimisation per retained region. This is a coverage minimum, not a claim that 256 points resolve the landscape. Screen Monte Carlo with at least 4,096 outer paths. For every scored option, target independently estimated `2*SE_MC_i <= s_i/4`, and separately require assessed time-grid, normaliser and quadrature/nested-inner bias contributions to total at most `s_i/4`. For VIX forward residuals apply the same fractions to the frozen forward-loss scale. Report actual half-spread-relative accuracy too, explicitly identifying quotes for which the fixed price floor dominates. These gates cover all scored quotes; there is no undefined liquidity exemption.

Coarse screening may carry unresolved numerical status, but final ranking and feasibility claims require the gates above. Increase sampling or refine the engine when they fail; if the declared resource budget is exhausted, retain the candidate as numerically unresolved rather than reducing its weight or discarding difficult quotes. Start at 2, 8 and 32 integration steps per trading day and refine further as required, accounting for the feedback bank up to 5,000 inverse years as well as the Stage-3 spike bank. Compare 12-, 20- and 32-node per-axis Gaussian quadrature (or a converged sparse rule), refining maturity integration independently. For nested Monte Carlo, start an inner-count ladder such as 128, 512 and 2,048 continuations per outer state and extend it as needed. Use coupled inner prefixes to estimate changes with inner count on fixed outer states, independent replications to assess uncertainty in those changes, and outer-count refinement separately. One statistically inconclusive difference is not proof that inner bias is negligible; require repeated refinement or an independently converged quadrature comparison. Record any extrapolation or bias correction and validate it before use.

Benchmark both VIX engines on a small fixed set of central and tail states before launching the full search, recording wall time and memory at matched error. Stream outer/inner batches and cache reusable Gaussian quantities; avoid materialising all nested paths. Reprice finalists with at least three independent final seeds and larger outer/inner or quadrature budgets as appropriate. Save all path counts, failed convergence gates, assessed bias and timing. A standard error at fixed inner count is not a complete numerical-error report.

An unconstrained weighted $L_P+w_SL_{SPX}+w_VL_{VIX}+w_FL_{Futures}+R$ is a useful comparison. The P-constrained version is the primary analysis because the physical-fit tolerance is directly interpretable. Boundaries, regularisation and numerical budgets belong in the configuration, not in ad hoc code branches.

## Identification, ablations, and validation

Compute a finite-difference or automatic-differentiation Jacobian of *uncertainty-scaled* independent residuals at finalists, with enough numerical accuracy that derivative noise is smaller than target uncertainty. Report singular values, near-null parameter combinations, one-dimensional profiles and boundary hits. In particular inspect $q_Z\beta\rho_F$, $q_J\beta_J\rho_J$, state versus shift, and Q mean shift versus vertex displacement. Parameter vectors differing strongly along a flat direction should be reported as a region, not a unique estimate. Compare market residual improvements with independent numerical error and quote width.

Use the deterministic split from preprocessing: ranks 4 modulo 10 for model-selection validation, ranks 9 modulo 10 for final testing, and the whole 119-day SPX maturity for final maturity testing. The primary $\xi_0$ construction and any profiled curve use training data only, excluding both put/call source rows at held-out strikes. Keep all four VIX forwards in fitting and assess them separately; VIX option validation is therefore conditional on those shared forward inputs. A separate sensitivity may build $\xi_0$ from all quotes, but then **both strike and maturity** scores must be labelled conditional validation. Freeze architecture, penalties, risk-price scenarios and other selection choices before opening final-test results. Changes prompted by those results make subsequent evaluation on that set exploratory. No claim of out-of-time validity is possible with one option date. Once a panel exists, use rolling dates with shared structural parameters and date-specific states/inputs.

Only after inspecting a stable M2 residual, perform paired exact-zero comparisons with M2+O and M3 under the same market targets, primitive paths, initial candidates and numerical accuracy tolerances. M4 follows only if the separate components have material benefit. For this pilot, select optional layers using the model-selection validation strikes and numerical uncertainty; reserve final-test strikes and maturity for evaluation after selection. Require temporal validation once additional dates exist. Consider changing rate-grid shape before adding readout degree when roughness is the deficiency.

Primary output metrics: fraction of quotes priced inside their bid/ask intervals; outside-interval distance in price and in spreads, by instrument/maturity/moneyness; arbitrage-free minimum-violation benchmarks and flagged blocks; VIX forward absolute errors and cross-market second-moment checks; physical target values with empirical intervals, disjoint-period differences, proxy sensitivities, matched rolling simulation envelopes and joint-error feasibility thresholds; state/curve penalties; P/Q cap fractions; (M_Q) integration error; independent martingale and forward-variance checks; Jacobian profiles; validation/final-test errors with their information boundary; and seed/discretisation variation. For nested Monte Carlo separate inner bias, outer sampling error and shared normaliser error. Never use a single pooled RMSE as the sole result.
