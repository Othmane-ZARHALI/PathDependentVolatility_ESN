2. Why current A2 is not yet enough

Fat tails

For large positive \(x\),

\[\operatorname{softplus}(x)\sim x.\]

Since the unbounded A2 contribution is at most quadratic in Gaussian \(R_t\), its asymptotic volatility and return tails are stretched exponential rather than regularly varying. Around the normal operating region, where softplus behaves approximately exponentially, A2 can mimic a power law over a finite empirical range—but it does not possess an exact asymptotic tail index.

This is not necessarily a problem. I would define the primary requirement as matching empirical Hill plots, kurtosis and tail probabilities over observable quantiles, rather than insisting on an asymptotic theorem that the data cannot reliably identify.

The current softplus readout is nonlinear in the parameters. Even though \(R\) and \(Z\) are reusable, every parameter change requires recomputing

\[\sigma_t,\qquad \int_0^T \sigma_t\,dB_t,\qquad \int_0^T \sigma_t^2\,dt,\]

and the VIX conditional expectation. No new random variables are required, but the entire volatility/price functional must be reevaluated.

More seriously, A2 has no complete:

  • variance-risk-premium specification;

3. The modification I recommend

I would retain the exogenous A2 feature generator, but make the volatility linear in a fixed feature dictionary.

Let

\[\Phi_t = \Phi(R_t,Z_t)\]

contain, for example:

\[1,\qquad a^\top R_t,\qquad (a^\top R_t-\beta)^2,\qquad Z_{1,t},\ldots,Z_{N_z,t},\]

together with selected Hermite or signature features of fast and slow OU projections.

Then define a signed diffusion coefficient

\[\sigma_t(\theta) = \sqrt{\xi_0(t)} \frac{\theta^\top\Phi_t} {\sqrt{\theta^\top C_t\theta}}, \qquad C_t=\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q} \left[\Phi_t\Phi_t^\top\right].\]

The instantaneous variance is

\[V_t=\sigma_t(\theta)^2\geq0.\]

The fact that \(\sigma_t\) itself can be signed is mathematically harmless: the observable variance is its square. This is precisely the principle behind Gaussian polynomial volatility models.

This change is powerful because, after normalizing \(\theta\), one can precompute

\[I_T = \int_0^T\sqrt{\xi_0(t)}\,\Phi_t\,dB_t,\]

and

\[Q_T = \int_0^T\xi_0(t)\,\Phi_t\Phi_t^\top\,dt.\]

Then

\[\log\frac{S_T}{F_T} = \theta^\top I_T -\frac12\theta^\top Q_T\theta.\]

No state or price simulation is required when \(\theta\) changes: only a linear and a quadratic form are evaluated.

Similarly,

\[\operatorname{VIX}_T^2 = \theta^\top M_T\theta,\]

where

\[M_T = \frac1\Delta \int_T^{T+\Delta} \xi_0(u)\, \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q} \left[ \Phi_u\Phi_u^\top\mid\mathcal F_T \right]du.\]

The matrices \(M_T\) can be computed:

  • analytically through polynomial-generator or signature methods if \(\Phi\) is polynomial/signature based; or
  • offline using conditional-expectation regression if the original nonlinear A2 \(Z\)-layer is retained.

This is exactly the form of offline/online separation exploited by Cuchiero et al.: signature samples are computed once, while SPX and VIX quantities become algebraic functions of the calibrated coefficients. They report joint SPX/VIX calibration using three OU primary factors and third-order signatures. Cuchiero, Gazzani, Möller and Svaluto-Ferro

Add one independent OU tail factor,

\[T_q(O_t)=\exp(qO_t^2-c_q), \qquad q<\frac14.\]

Use

with the return-correlated components of \(\Phi_t\) bounded.

\[\mathbb P(T_q(O_t)>x) \asymp x^{-1/(2q)}(\log x)^{-1/2}.\]

Thus \(q\) directly controls a genuine tail index, while \(q<1/4\) ensures the required second moment. 6. Consistency between \(\mathbb P\) and \(\mathbb Q\)

A single model should not use unrelated readout coefficients under the two measures. I recommend:

  • shared across measures: OU rates, feature map, correlations, reservoir matrices, readout \(\theta\), and optional tail parameter \(q\);
  • \(\mathbb P\)-specific: equity drift;
  • \(\mathbb Q\)-specific: market prices of state risk and today’s forward-variance input curve \(\xi_0\);
  • initial-condition specific: current OU/reservoir state.

With deterministic or piecewise-constant market prices of risk, the \(\mathbb Q\) OU paths equal the same centered Gaussian paths plus deterministic mean shifts. This preserves the strongest practical form of path reuse. More general state-dependent premia should be treated as outer hyperparameters because they change the state evolution.

Recommended exponential-quadratic A2 readout

Let \(R_t\) be the fixed exogenous OU lift and introduce two projections:

\[Y_t=a^\top R_t, \qquad F_t=f^\top R_t.\]

Here \(Y\) can represent the slower rough/trend component, while \(F\) is a faster component intended primarily for VIX spikes. Define

\[\eta_t = \ell Y_t + q_Z(Y_t-\beta)^2 + q_F F_t^2 + d^\top Z_t,\]

where \(Z_t\) is the existing bounded A2 recurrent layer. The instantaneous variance would be

\[V_t = \xi_0(t) \frac{\exp(2\eta_t)} {\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[\exp(2\eta_t)]}.\]

The normalization implies

\[\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[V_t]=\xi_0(t),\]

so the input forward-variance curve is matched exactly.

I would initially omit \(d^\top Z_t\) and add it only if the bounded ESN layer materially improves the physical-measure diagnostics. The OU lift plus shifted quadratic terms may already contain most of the useful structure.

Why the shifted quadratic is important

Expanding it gives

\[q_Z(Y_t-\beta)^2 = q_ZY_t^2-2q_Z\beta Y_t+q_Z\beta^2.\]

It therefore contains:

  • an even quadratic component, producing convex volatility feedback and Zumbach-type effects;
  • a linear component, which can produce directional asymmetry and leverage;
  • strong state-dependent volatility-of-volatility without a fifth-degree term.

The shift \(\beta\) is not merely cosmetic. An unshifted quadratic treats positive and negative trends identically and therefore cannot by itself generate the desired return/volatility asymmetry.

Your observation about spike decay is correct

Suppose a fast OU factor receives a shock \(F_0=f_0\). Its conditional deterministic decay is approximately

\[F_s \approx f_0e^{-\lambda_Fs}.\]

Its quadratic contribution to log-variance is then

\[q_FF_s^2 = q_Ff_0^2e^{-2\lambda_Fs}.\]

Ignoring the other terms temporarily,

\[\frac{V_s}{\bar V_s} \approx \exp\!\left(2q_Ff_0^2e^{-2\lambda_Fs}\right).\]

The logarithmic decay rate is

\[\frac{d}{ds} \log\!\left(\frac{V_s}{\bar V_s}\right) = -4\lambda_Fq_Ff_0^2e^{-2\lambda_Fs}.\]

Thus the decline is steepest immediately after the spike and becomes progressively slower. This is precisely the qualitative shape you described.

Using several OU rates gives still more control:

\[F_t=\sum_{j=1}^{m_F}f_jR_t^{(j)}.\]

Fast factors determine the initial collapse, while slower factors create the shoulder and persistence. This is a better mechanism than asking a single quintic polynomial to produce both the amplitude and the temporal shape.

VIX remains analytically manageable

If the readout depends only on Gaussian OU states, then conditionally on \(\mathcal F_T\),

\[R_u\mid\mathcal F_T \sim \mathcal N(m_{T,u},\Sigma_{T,u}).\]

Conditional exponential-quadratic moments are available explicitly. In general,

\[\mathbb E\!\left[ e^{X^\top AX+b^\top X+c} \right] = \frac{ \exp\!\left( c+m^\top Am+b^\top m+ \frac12(2Am+b)^\top (I-2\Sigma A)^{-1} \Sigma(2Am+b) \right) }{ \sqrt{\det(I-2\Sigma A)} },\]

provided

\[I-2\Sigma A\succ0.\]

Therefore,

\[\operatorname{VIX}_T^2 = \frac{1}{\Delta} \int_T^{T+\Delta} \xi_0(u) \frac{ \mathbb E_T^{\mathbb Q}[e^{2\eta_u}] }{ \mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[e^{2\eta_u}] } \,du\]

can be computed using deterministic maturity quadrature, without nested Monte Carlo.

If the nonlinear recurrent state \(Z_t\) is included, this exact formula is generally lost. Path reuse still works, but the conditional VIX map would need either regression, quantization, or low-dimensional quadrature.

What “path reuse” would mean here

For fixed OU hyperparameters, simulate once and cache:

\[R,\qquad W^S,\qquad Z\ \text{if retained}.\]

When \(\ell,q_Z,\beta,q_F\) or other readout parameters change:

  • the exogenous trajectories and random numbers are not resimulated;
  • \(\eta_t\), \(V_t\), the stock integral and payoffs are recomputed;
  • VIX uses analytic conditional moments or cached-state quadrature.

The exponential link prevents the stock integral from being a simple polynomial in the parameters. Nevertheless, recalculating an exponential over cached arrays is far cheaper and statistically smoother than resimulating the state process.

A deterministic market price of risk can shift the OU conditional means between \(\mathbb P\) and \(\mathbb Q\) while preserving the underlying Gaussian innovations, so the same cached innovations can support both measures.

The main caveat: moment and martingale admissibility

A positive quadratic exponent cannot be arbitrarily large. For the scalar example,

\[\eta_t=qX_t^2, \qquad X_t\sim\mathcal N(0,s_t^2),\]

the variance expectation requires

\[1-4qs_t^2>0.\]

In the multivariate case, the corresponding spectral condition is

\[I-4\Sigma_t^{1/2}Q\Sigma_t^{1/2}\succ0.\]

These constraints must be enforced throughout calibration. Finite instantaneous moments alone also do not automatically prove that the discounted stock is a true martingale.

Because asymptotic power tails are not essential to your objective, a robust alternative is to apply a smooth upper tempering function:

\[V_t = \xi_0(t) \frac{\exp\!\left(2h_M(\eta_t)\right)} {\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q} [\exp\!\left(2h_M(\eta_t)\right)]},\]

where \(h_M(x)\approx x\) over the empirically relevant region but approaches an upper bound for extreme \(x\). This preserves observable fat tails and spike behaviour while making integrability and the martingale argument substantially safer. Exact Gaussian moment formulas would then be replaced by inexpensive low-dimensional quadrature.

Roughness is retained

Suppose that over the relevant range of scales,

\[\mathbb E\left[(Y_{t+\Delta}-Y_t)^2\right] \sim C\Delta^{2H}.\]

For a sufficiently smooth readout \(g\),

\[g(Y_{t+\Delta})-g(Y_t) = g'(Y_t)(Y_{t+\Delta}-Y_t) + o\!\left(\Delta^H\right).\]

Consequently, as long as \(g'(Y_t)\neq0\), the transformed volatility has the same local roughness exponent \(H\). This applies to both

\[g(y)=c+q(y-\beta)^2\]

and

\[g(y)=\exp\!\left(2q(y-\beta)^2\right).\]

At the parabola vertex \(y=\beta\), the first derivative vanishes, but this does not normally change the estimated global exponent because hitting the vertex at a fixed time has probability zero under a continuous state distribution.

The usual qualification remains: a finite OU lift reproduces roughness only over its designed frequency range. It does not produce a genuinely fractional small-time asymptotic unless the number and range of OU factors are taken to an appropriate limit.

Leverage and Zumbach are preserved

Let the feedback projection be

\[Y_t=a^\top R_t^{F},\]

where \(R^F\) is driven by a standardised shock correlated with the stock innovation:

\[dW_t^F = \rho_F\,dW_t^S + \sqrt{1-\rho_F^2}\,dB_t.\]

The shifted quadratic term is

\[q_Z(Y_t-\beta)^2 = q_ZY_t^2-2q_Z\beta Y_t+q_Z\beta^2.\]

The roles are distinct:

  • \(q_ZY_t^2\) generates the nonlinear time-reversal asymmetry associated with the strong Zumbach effect;
  • \(-2q_Z\beta Y_t\), together with \(\rho_F\), generates leverage;
  • \(\beta=0\) would remove the directional asymmetry of the parabola.

Thus both mechanisms survive when the quintic is removed. Indeed, the Gate C ablation found that the quadratic term—not the recurrent A2 layer or the activity block—was the decisive Zumbach mechanism.

One qualification is that changing the outer link from exponential to a direct quadratic variance readout can change the magnitude. The mechanism survives, but the Gate C numerical value cannot simply be assumed to remain unchanged.

Fat tails are retained, but differently

With the direct quadratic model,

\[V_t = \xi_0(t) \frac{c+q(Y_t-\beta)^2} {\mathbb E[c+q(Y_t-\beta)^2]},\]

With the exponential-quadratic model,

\[V_t = \xi_0(t) \frac{\exp\!\left(2\eta_t\right)} {\mathbb E[\exp(2\eta_t)]},\]

Once a smooth upper cap is imposed, the model should not be claimed to have asymptotic power-law returns. That is consistent with your stated objective: reproduce the empirical body and accessible tail range, rather than enforce a particular asymptotic law.

The Taylor effect,

\[\operatorname{Corr}(|r_t|,|r_{t+k}|) > \operatorname{Corr}(r_t^2,r_{t+k}^2),\]

is not guaranteed by roughness, leverage or Zumbach.

The Gate D analysis indicated a dispersion threshold. For example, at representative volatility persistence, the standard deviation of log-volatility had to be approximately

\[\operatorname{sd}(\log \sigma_t)\gtrsim 0.23\text{--}0.28\]
